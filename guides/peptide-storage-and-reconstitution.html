Peptide storage and reconstitution: temperatures, buffers and freeze-thaw

Storage is where most peptide failures happen. This guide gives the temperatures, the buffers and the four handling mistakes that show up in assay data.

Updated 27 Nov 2026 · Reviewed by the Peflag QC desk · Release method

Short answer. Lyophilized peptide powder keeps for 36 months at -20 °C protected from light, and short ambient transit does not damage it. Once reconstituted, aliquot and store at -70 to -80 °C, and avoid more than one freeze-thaw cycle.

Storage matrix

Dry powder lives at -20 °C; reconstituted peptide lives at -70 to -80 °C in single-use aliquots. Anything in between is a compromise with a short clock on it.

Why lyophilized powder survives a five-day journey

Water is what drives peptide degradation, and lyophilization removes it. With water content held below 5.0%, hydrolysis and deamidation slow by orders of magnitude, so a sealed vial at ambient temperature is not the same risk as a solution at ambient temperature.

What does damage powder in transit is a broken seal or a punctured stopper — which is why vials ship in a rigid, shock-absorbing tray with desiccant rather than a padded envelope.

Reconstitution without creating aggregates

Add solvent down the vial wall, swirl gently, and never vortex. Shear is the single most common cause of avoidable aggregation in a laboratory stock.

  • Solvent: PBS pH 7.4 for most assays; water only if the protocol calls for it.
  • Concentration: keep stocks at or below 1 mg/mL where the assay allows — lipidated peptides in particular film on plastic at higher concentrations.
  • Technique: pipette the buffer down the wall, then roll the vial between the palms rather than shaking.
  • Clarity: a slightly cloudy solution after 10 minutes usually means the concentration is too high, not that the peptide is degraded.

Arrival checks, in order

  1. Count the vials against the packing list and check the batch number matches the COA.
  2. Confirm each vial is intact and the desiccant is still active.
  3. Let sealed vials equilibrate to room temperature before opening.
  4. Store at -20 °C within the day; do not leave powder in a warm room "for later".

Summer versus winter shipping

Seasonal risk is about time in a hot or freezing depot, not about the month. A two-day express parcel into a temperature-controlled facility is low risk in July; a two-week economy parcel is a risk in any month.

  • Summer: avoid economy services and weekend deliveries that sit in a depot. The powder tolerates ambient air; the seal and the tray are what suffer.
  • Winter: freezing in transit is tolerable for dry powder, but condensation on opening is not — let the sealed vial warm up first.

Freezer hygiene

Frost-free freezers cycle above -10 °C and are unsuitable for peptide storage. A manual-defrost unit holds a stable temperature; a household fridge-freezer does not.

Keep vials in a sealed secondary container with desiccant, label by batch rather than by date, and never store powder next to open solvent bottles. A vial left at room temperature for a day should be aliquoted and used, not returned to the stock box.

Buyer questions

Peptide storage and reconstitution — questions buyers ask

Does ambient shipping damage lyophilized peptide?

No, not over a normal transit window. Lyophilized powder is stable at ambient temperature for the days an express parcel spends in transit; damage comes from moisture ingress and repeated freeze-thaw after arrival, not from a five-day journey.

Should I reconstitute with water or buffer?

Use the buffer your protocol specifies; use water only when the assay tolerates it. Salts matter for peptides that aggregate or adsorb, and the wrong solvent is a common cause of a cloudy solution.

How many freeze-thaw cycles are acceptable?

One. Aliquot before the first freeze so each tube is opened once. Repeated cycling raises the high-molecular-weight fraction, which shows up on SEC even when HPLC purity looks unchanged.

Why -80 °C rather than -20 °C for working aliquots?

-70 to -80 °C slows aggregation and protease activity in solution; -20 °C is for dry powder. A frost-free freezer that cycles above -10 °C is not suitable for either.

What should I check when the parcel arrives?

Check the vial count, the batch number against the COA, and that each vial is intact and desiccated. Let sealed vials reach room temperature before opening to stop condensation forming on cold powder.
