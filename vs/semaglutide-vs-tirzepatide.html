Semaglutide vs tirzepatide: one receptor or two?

The most common comparison in incretin research, and the one most often reduced to a single misleading word: potency.

Updated 27 Nov 2026 · Reviewed by the Peflag QC desk · Release method

Short answer. Semaglutide is GLP-1 selective with roughly 165 hours of half-life; tirzepatide adds GIP-receptor agonism and runs about five days. The practical difference is whether a study needs a single-incretin control or a dual comparator.

Structural signature

Semaglutide carries an Aib8 substitution and a C18 diacid on an AEEA-AEEA linker; tirzepatide carries Aib2/Aib13 and a C20 diacid on Lys20. Both bind albumin, but the linkers and acyl chains differ, which is why their solubility behaviour in the vial is not identical.

Potency claims need care

Comparing potency across receptor selectivity is only meaningful at the receptor you measured. An EC50 at GLP-1R does not describe what the GIP arm contributes, and milligram-matched doses are not molar-matched doses.

For a fair comparison, plan molar concentrations, run both compounds on the same assay day, and include a vehicle arm.

Designing a head-to-head

Three arms are the minimum: semaglutide, tirzepatide and vehicle. A fourth arm with a GIP-receptor antagonist is what lets you attribute a difference to the GIP arm rather than to more total incretin signal.

  • Match molar concentrations, not milligrams.
  • Run both compounds on the same assay day with the same vehicle.
  • Pre-register the primary read-out — weight and glycaemia move on different timescales.

What the comparison can and cannot show

It can show that a dual agonist produces a different response from a single agonist at matched exposure. It cannot show which receptor is responsible without an antagonist arm, and it cannot establish clinical relevance.

Buyer questions

Semaglutide vs tirzepatide — questions buyers ask

Is tirzepatide simply a stronger semaglutide?

No. It engages a second receptor. A higher dose of semaglutide increases GLP-1R signalling; tirzepatide adds GIPR signalling, which is a different pharmacological intervention.

Which has the longer half-life?

Tirzepatide, at about five days, against roughly 165 hours for semaglutide. Both support long-interval dosing in research models, with different washout behaviour between crossover arms.

Which one is the better control?

Semaglutide is the standard GLP-1-selective control; tirzepatide is the dual comparator. Most modern designs use both, plus vehicle.

Do they differ analytically?

Yes, by mass and by sequence. Semaglutide is 4113.58 g/mol and tirzepatide 4813.50 g/mol, and the chromatographic retention differs enough that a mixed sample is detectable.
