Cagrilintide vs CagriSema: single amylin analogue or 1:1 combination

One is a receptor tool, the other is a fixed-ratio combination. Choosing wrong costs you the ability to attribute the effect.

Updated 27 Nov 2026 · Reviewed by the Peflag QC desk · Release method

Short answer. Cagrilintide is a lipidated amylin analogue acting on CTR/RAMP complexes; CagriSema pairs it 1:1 with semaglutide in the same kit. Use the single agent to isolate amylin signalling and the combination to test additivity.

Why the ratio matters more than the dose

In a combination product, the exposure ratio between the two components is the variable that changes the result. A 1:1 kit keeps that ratio fixed while the vial strength changes.

How the combination is analysed

Both peptides are quantified in one reversed-phase HPLC run and reported as two purities plus the measured ratio. Cagrilintide and semaglutide elute at different retention times, so the COA does not assume 1:1 from fill weight.

Designing the additivity study

Four arms are the minimum that answers the question. Anything less measures the combination without telling you what it adds.

  • Arm 1: cagrilintide alone, matched molar exposure.
  • Arm 2: semaglutide alone, matched molar exposure.
  • Arm 3: CagriSema at the corresponding strength.
  • Arm 4: vehicle, identical across all arms.

If independent titration of the two components is needed, order them as separate compounds rather than splitting the kit — the ratio is the point of the kit.

When splitting the kit is acceptable

Split the kit only when both components are assayed at the same dilution and the ratio is re-checked by HPLC. Otherwise order the two compounds separately.

Splitting introduces two errors: a pipetting ratio error and a differential adsorption error, because the two lipidated peptides bind plastic differently. That is tolerable in a qualitative pilot and fatal to an additivity claim.

Reading the two-peak chromatogram

A CagriSema COA should show two main peaks at separate retention times plus a reported ratio. One peak means the method did not resolve the components; two peaks outside the ratio tolerance means the fill is not 1:1.

Buyer questions

Cagrilintide vs CagriSema — questions buyers ask

Is CagriSema just cagrilintide plus semaglutide mixed at the bench?

No. It is supplied as a fixed 1:1 kit with both peptides co-filled and both quantified on the same COA. Bench mixing introduces a ratio error that a fixed kit avoids.

Which one should be used to isolate amylin signalling?

Cagrilintide. CagriSema includes semaglutide, so it cannot separate the amylin contribution from the GLP-1 contribution.

What is the minimal additivity study?

Four arms: cagrilintide alone, semaglutide alone, CagriSema, and vehicle. Without the two single-agent arms the combination result has nothing to be additive to.

Does the ratio change between strengths?

No. Every kit holds equal milligrams of both peptides, so the 1:1 ratio is constant across 2.5+2.5, 5+5 and 10+10 mg.
