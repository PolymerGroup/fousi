Tirzepatide vs retatrutide: dual GIP/GLP-1 or triple agonist?

Two 39-residue peptides, one receptor apart. The difference decides whether a study measures incretin action alone or incretin plus glucagon.

Updated 27 Nov 2026 · Reviewed by the Peflag QC desk · Release method

Short answer. Tirzepatide engages GIP and GLP-1 receptors; retatrutide adds glucagon-receptor agonism on a related C20-diacid architecture. Both are 39-residue peptides, and their masses separate them analytically.

At a glance

Both are 39-residue incretin peptides built on a C20-diacid architecture; the difference is whether the glucagon receptor is engaged. The table gives the figures a study plan needs.

Structural differences

Tirzepatide uses Aib substitutions at positions 2 and 13 with a C20 diacid on Lys20; retatrutide keeps the C20 diacid but adds glucagon-receptor agonism through its N-terminal and mid-chain residues. Both stabilise albumin binding, which is where the multi-day exposure comes from.

Practically, the two behave similarly in solution and differ in pharmacology. Handling notes that apply to one generally apply to the other — slow dissolution, adsorption at low concentration, and sensitivity to shear.

Which one for which study

Choose tirzepatide when the question is about dual incretin action; choose retatrutide when the glucagon arm is part of the hypothesis.

Handling differences that matter

Retatrutide solutions are light-sensitive because of the conjugated fatty acid; tirzepatide is the slower of the two to dissolve at neutral pH. Neither tolerates vortexing.

  • Tirzepatide: add buffer in two or three steps and let the vial stand; the solution clears without shear.
  • Retatrutide: foil-wrap working solutions; filter through 0.22 µm if any particle remains after standing.
  • Both: aliquot before freezing — a single refreeze measurably raises the high-molecular-weight fraction.

Cost per study arm

Budget the arm, not the vial. A dual-versus-triple comparison needs four arms, so the number that matters is the cost of the full set, not the price of one kit.

  • A minimum design needs tirzepatide, retatrutide, a GLP-1-selective compound and vehicle.
  • Kits hold 10 vials; a pilot arm usually consumes two to four, so one kit covers a pilot and a repeat.
  • Tiered pricing starts at five kits — the point where a multi-arm design becomes cheaper per arm.

Moving a study from one to the other

Changing compound mid-programme resets the exposure baseline. Moving from tirzepatide to retatrutide means re-baselining the washout window, the molar dose and the primary read-out before the new arms can be compared with the old ones.

Buyer questions

Tirzepatide vs retatrutide — questions buyers ask

Which is more potent, tirzepatide or retatrutide?

They cannot be ranked by potency alone, because they do not engage the same receptors. Retatrutide adds glucagon-receptor agonism, so a potency comparison at GLP-1R answers a narrower question than the pharmacology does.

Can the two be told apart analytically?

Yes, by mass. Tirzepatide free base is 4813.50 g/mol and retatrutide is 4731.33 g/mol, so a deconvoluted ESI-MS result separates them unambiguously.

Do they have the same half-life?

No. Tirzepatide is about five days and retatrutide about six. The difference matters for washout between crossover arms more than for single-dose work.

Which one should be the comparator in a triple-agonist study?

Tirzepatide is the usual dual comparator, with a GLP-1-selective compound as the single comparator. That three-arm structure isolates what each additional receptor contributes.
