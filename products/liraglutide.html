What Liraglutide is

Liraglutide shares the GLP-1 (7-37) backbone with semaglutide but uses simpler chemistry: a single Arg34Lys26 substitution and a C16 palmitic acid attached through a γ-glutamate linker. That gives moderate albumin affinity and a half-life of about 13 hours — long enough for once-daily dosing, short enough for a comparatively fast washout in crossover studies.

It remains one of the best-characterised GLP-1 receptor agonists in the literature, which makes it attractive for teaching-laboratory work, for receptor-pharmacology practicals and for studies where a shorter exposure window is methodologically preferable to a weekly depot.

Mechanism of action

Liraglutide binds GLP-1R and activates Gs/cAMP signalling, potentiating glucose-dependent insulin release and suppressing glucagon. Albumin binding is weaker and more dynamic than with C18/C20 analogues, so free fraction fluctuates across the dosing interval. It is degraded by DPP-4 only slowly, thanks to the palmitoyl-mediated albumin shielding.

Sourcing note. If your protocol needs a short washout between crossover arms, the ~13-hour half-life is the practical reason to choose this over a weekly analogue.

Research applications

These are the four uses this compound is most often ordered for in laboratory work:

  • Daily exposure profile. A shorter half-life than semaglutide simplifies washout and crossover designs.
  • Simple acylation chemistry. C16 palmitate rather than a large diacid linker — relevant for SAR teaching and comparison.
  • Extensive literature. Decades of published pharmacology make it a reliable reference compound.
  • Three strengths. 5 mg, 10 mg and 30 mg vials cover receptor assays through chronic dosing studies.

Handling notes for Liraglutide

Three practical differences matter when this compound is handled, compared with other peptides in the catalogue:

  • Palmitate. The C16 chain lowers aqueous solubility; allow longer dissolution at neutral pH.
  • Exposure. A ~13 h half-life makes it the practical choice when a short washout is needed.
  • Handling. Avoid foaming; the acylated peptide films on plastic.

Why choose liraglutide over semaglutide for a study?
Exposure kinetics. If your protocol needs a daily profile, a faster washout between crossover arms, or a compound with a very large published literature, liraglutide is the simpler tool. Weekly analogues suit chronic endpoints better.
Is the 30 mg vial intended for a single dose?
No — these are research-grade lyophilized vials, not patient-ready products. The 30 mg size exists so a lab can prepare a larger working stock from one traced batch instead of pooling several small vials.

Ask for the current batch COA — it is sent before payment, not after. The document carries the batch number, an HPLC chromatogram with area-normalised purity, an ESI-MS spectrum with the deconvoluted mass, Karl Fischer water content and the fill-weight range. If your QA group requires independent confirmation, we can arrange third-party testing at an ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratory and issue the report in your institution's name.
