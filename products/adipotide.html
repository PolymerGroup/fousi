What Adipotide is

Adipotide — also catalogued as FTPP — is a two-part molecule. The N-terminal sequence CKGGRAKDC binds prohibitin, a protein enriched on the endothelial surface of white adipose tissue vasculature. The C-terminal D(KLAKLAK)2 sequence is a cationic pro-apoptotic peptide that disrupts mitochondrial membranes once internalised. The homing motif concentrates the apoptotic payload on adipose endothelium, which is the basis for its use in adipose-remodelling studies.

In research models the compound has been used to examine whether targeted ablation of white-adipose vasculature changes fat mass, insulin sensitivity and energy balance. It is a mechanistically distinct tool from incretin agonists and lipolytic fragments: the target is the tissue's blood supply rather than the adipocyte itself.

Mechanism of action

The homing sequence binds prohibitin on adipose endothelial cells; after internalisation, the cationic KLAKLAK repeat permeabilises mitochondrial membranes and triggers apoptosis in the targeted endothelium. Loss of local vasculature is followed by adipocyte death and adipose tissue remodelling. Because the effect depends on prohibitin accessibility, results are sensitive to tissue preparation and model choice.

Sourcing note. The D-amino-acid effector segment is the part most often truncated during synthesis; our release data reports the full-length ratio wherever the method resolves it.

Research applications

These are the four uses this compound is most often ordered for in laboratory work:

  • Vascular target. Acts on adipose endothelium rather than on adipocytes or incretin receptors — a genuinely different mechanism.
  • Chimeric architecture. Homing motif plus apoptotic effector in one synthesised peptide, so both modules are present in a fixed ratio.
  • D-amino-acid payload. The D(KLAKLAK)2 block resists proteolysis and retains mitochondrial activity.
  • Three strengths. 2, 5 and 10 mg vials suit pilot work through chronic studies.

Handling notes for Adipotide

Three practical differences matter when this compound is handled, compared with other peptides in the catalogue:

  • D-amino acids. The effector segment is protease-resistant; the full-length ratio is reported on the COA.
  • Charge. Highly cationic and binds anionic surfaces — use low-binding labware.
  • Solubility. Soluble in water; filter before cell work.

Why does adipotide appear under both 'Adipotide' and 'FTPP'?
FTPP is the descriptive name (fat-targeting pro-apoptotic peptide) and adipotide is the common catalogue name for the same sequence, CAS 859216-15-2. We consolidate them on one page to avoid duplicate listings for one molecule.
What documentation accompanies the adipotide kit?
HPLC purity, ESI-MS identity and net peptide content per vial. Because the molecule contains a D-amino-acid segment, we also report the ratio of the two modules where the analytical method allows, so partial truncation during synthesis is visible.
How do I verify purity and identity before ordering?

Ask for the current batch COA — it is sent before payment, not after. The document carries the batch number, an HPLC chromatogram with area-normalised purity, an ESI-MS spectrum with the deconvoluted mass, Karl Fischer water content and the fill-weight range. If your QA group requires independent confirmation, we can arrange third-party testing at an ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratory and issue the report in your institution's name.

How is Adipotide packed and how long does delivery take?

Stocked kits leave Jinan within 24–48 hours; express transit is typically 5–9 working days. Ten vials travel in a tamper-evident, shock-absorbing tray with desiccant inside a neutral outer carton that names no product. Tracking is issued at dispatch, and the parcel includes a commercial invoice, packing list, COA and MSDS.
