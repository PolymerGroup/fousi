What Tirzepatide is

Tirzepatide takes the GLP-1 backbone and re-engineers it into a balanced dual incretin agonist. The sequence carries Aib substitutions at positions 2 and 13 for protease resistance, a C-terminal amide, and a C20 fatty diacid on Lys20 via a γGlu linker that produces roughly five days of exposure in humans. The GIP receptor component adds insulinotropic activity and appears to modulate adipose lipid handling, while GLP-1R engagement drives the satiety and gastric-emptying effects that define this drug class.

In research settings tirzepatide is used to dissect GIPR versus GLP-1R contributions: receptor knock-out models, β-arrestin recruitment assays, adipocyte lipolysis and glucose-clamp studies all benefit from having a single molecule that engages both pathways. It is also the natural comparator when evaluating triple agonists such as retatrutide, because the two share a common backbone architecture and differ mainly by glucagon-receptor recruitment.

Mechanism of action

Tirzepatide engages GIPR and GLP-1R with different intrinsic efficacies and shows biased signalling at GLP-1R, favouring cAMP over β-arrestin recruitment relative to native GLP-1. Dual activation amplifies glucose-dependent insulin secretion, improves insulin sensitivity in peripheral tissue models, and reduces food intake through complementary hypothalamic circuits. The C20 diacid provides high albumin affinity and slow subcutaneous release, giving near-steady exposure between weekly administrations.

Research applications

These are the four uses this compound is most often ordered for in laboratory work:

  • Dual receptor coverage. A single peptide lets a study compare GIPR and GLP-1R contributions without running two dosing arms.
  • C20 fatty-acid acylation. Strong albumin binding underpins the ~5-day half-life and stable exposure in chronic models.
  • Biased GLP-1R signalling. Lower β-arrestin recruitment than native GLP-1 has made tirzepatide a standard tool in biased-agonism work.
  • Widest strength range. 2 mg to 120 mg per vial covers dose-ranging studies, analytical standards and bulk reformulation work.

Handling notes for Tirzepatide

Three practical differences matter when this compound is handled, compared with other peptides in the catalogue:

  • Slow dissolution. The C20 diacid drives self-assembly; add buffer in two or three steps and let the vial stand.
  • Adsorption. Dilute stocks keep better in low-binding tubes than in untreated glass.
  • Shear. Avoid vortexing and repeated pipetting; both increase aggregation in lipidated incretins.

Analytical release data

Do you supply both the 5 mg and the high-strength 120 mg tirzepatide kits?
Yes — the full ladder from 2 mg to 120 mg per vial is stocked. Larger vials are usually chosen for bulk reformulation or for labs that prepare a single working stock per study; the 5 mg and 10 mg kits remain the most common for receptor assays.
How is tirzepatide distinguished from retatrutide in your COA?
By mass. Tirzepatide free base is 4813.50 Da and retatrutide is 4731.33 Da; the ESI-MS spectrum on each COA shows the multiply charged envelope and the deconvoluted mass, so a mixed-up or blended batch cannot pass identity testing.
How do I verify purity and identity before ordering?

Ask for the current batch COA — it is sent before payment, not after. The document carries the batch number, an HPLC chromatogram with area-normalised purity, an ESI-MS spectrum with the deconvoluted mass, Karl Fischer water content and the fill-weight range. If your QA group requires independent confirmation, we can arrange third-party testing at an ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratory and issue the report in your institution's name.
